# Code of Conduct

ASEH is built through careful disagreement, source-based review, and respect for the people and communities represented by the project.

## Expected behavior

Contributors and maintainers are expected to:

- treat people with dignity across differences in background, identity, observance, practice, and expertise;
- critique claims, evidence, and implementation choices without attacking the person presenting them;
- represent disputed positions accurately and avoid dismissive or inflammatory language;
- disclose uncertainty, conflicts of interest, and the limits of one's expertise;
- protect private, sensitive, and personally identifying information;
- accept correction, credit sources and contributors, and repair harm promptly.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized attention, deliberate misgendering, doxxing, intimidation, sustained disruption, plagiarism, source fabrication, and retaliation against a good-faith reporter or reviewer are not permitted. Using religious, cultural, or technical authority to shame, coerce, or silence others is also unacceptable.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository activity, project communications, review spaces, and public settings where someone is representing ASEH.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns through the repository's [private reporting form](https://github.com/Gil-netizen/aseh/security/advisories/new) and begin the title with `Conduct report`. The form is enabled for this repository and provides a private channel to the owner, @Gil-netizen. If a report concerns the owner, use GitHub's platform reporting tools so the subject of the report does not control its handling. Do not include sensitive conduct reports in a public issue.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to the people needed to assess and address a report. They may remove content, issue a warning, restrict participation, or permanently exclude a participant based on the severity and pattern of behavior. Retaliation for a good-faith report is itself a violation.

Anyone named in a report must recuse themselves from deciding its outcome. Enforcement decisions should document the conduct at issue, the policy applied, and any action taken while protecting the reporter's privacy.
